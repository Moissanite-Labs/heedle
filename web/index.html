How to read it

  • Cloth: a network of 467 weights turns each point into a colour. Every input is on screen, so the picture is the model's whole behaviour.
  • Text: a network of 4,700 weights reads three characters and scores the next one. It was trained on Heedle's own documents. Each pick weaves a line from the new weights, on the same random draws as before, so a change in the text is a change in the model.
  • Pattern: the model's guess after each of its twelve commonest contexts. A larger square is a likelier character. Green rose, red fell.
  • Shafts: the directions in weight space that change the outputs most per step. To first order, one unit of travel changes the outputs by one unit, whichever shaft you move.
  • Treadles: named changes, each tied to the shafts. The percentage is the share of the named change these shafts can carry.
  • Heddles: inputs you pin. The loom is threaded around them, so pinned outputs do not shift to first order.

What a beat checks

  • Weft's own gate: the weights stay within a fixed distance of the baseline and the budget covers the pick.
  • A claim: no point of the cloth changes by more than the limit. A search looks for one point that breaks it, and it alone decides. If it finds one, the pick snaps and the cloth stays. Finding none is a sampled reading.
  • A claim: no context changes by more than the limit. Every one of the 21,952 contexts is read, and that alone decides. If one breaks the claim, the pick snaps and the model stays. Finding none is complete, up to rounding.
  • Fresh probes, drawn from the new weights' hash after the pick is fixed, measure what the pick did. They do not decide it.
  • The closer a pick comes to its limits, the longer you must hold to make it.

The record

Every beat, snap and unpick is chained by hash into a draft. Anyone can replay it without this page: python3 -m heedle verify draft.json
